# AIDIS Data Assessment: Survey Structure & Formal vs Informal Credit Evidence

**Document File:** `data/raw/aidis/All India Debt and Investment Survey - 2019.pdf`  
**Publication Title:** NSS Report No. 588 (77/18.2): *All India Debt & Investment Survey - 2019*  
**Issuing Authority:** National Statistical Office (NSO), Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI), Government of India  
**Release Date:** September 2021  
**Total Pages:** 1,919  

---

## 1. Document Classification & Nature
* **Type of Material:** **Official Published Statistical Report & Comprehensive Aggregate Statements** (including 1,753 pages of detailed tables in Appendix A).
* **Is it microdata?** **NO.** This document is **not** a raw unit-level CSV/STATA/SPSS microdata file. It contains aggregate weighted survey estimates, state-by-state tabulations, and statistical distribution statements compiled from the unit survey records.
* **Survey Round:** NSS 77th Round.
* **Survey Period:** January 2019 – December 2019 (Visit 1: Jan–Aug 2019; Visit 2: Sep–Dec 2019).
* **Reference Date for Debt & Liabilities:** **30.06.2018**.
* **Geography:** All-India coverage across all States and Union Territories.
* **Rural/Urban Representation:** Stratified multistage design surveying rural villages and urban blocks separately.

---

## 2. Survey Concepts & Official Classification Terminology

The survey classifies credit agencies strictly into two categories:

### A. Institutional Credit Agencies
1. **Government**
2. **Co-operative Society / Co-operative Bank**
3. **Commercial Bank (including Regional Rural Banks - RRBs)**
4. **Insurance Company**
5. **Provident Fund**
6. **Financial Corporation / Institution**
7. **Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs)**
8. **Self-Help Group - Bank Linked (SHG-BL)**
9. **Self-Help Group - Non-Bank Linked (SHG-NBL) / Joint Liability Group (JLG)**
10. **Other Institutional Agencies**

### B. Non-Institutional Credit Agencies (Informal Sources)
1. **Landlord**
2. **Agricultural Moneylender**
3. **Professional Moneylender**
4. **Input Supplier**
5. **Relatives and Friends**
6. **Chit Fund**
7. **Market Commission Agent / Traders**
8. **Others (Doctors, Lawyers, etc.)**

## 3. Empirical Findings for Rural Credit (All-India vs. Karnataka)

### 3.1 Incidence of Indebtedness (IOI) as on 30.06.2018
* **All-India Rural Average (Statement 3.11, Page 51):**
  * Overall Rural Households: **35.0%**
  * Cultivator Households: **40.3%**
  * Non-Cultivator Households: **28.2%**
* **Karnataka Rural (Statement 7, Page 92):**
  * Overall Rural Households: **48.1%** (significantly higher debt incidence than national average)
  * Cultivator Households: **59.2%**
  * Non-Cultivator Rural Households: **32.9%**

### 3.2 Share of Outstanding Cash Debt: Institutional vs. Non-Institutional (Statement 8, Page 94)
* **All-India Rural:**
  * Institutional Agencies: **66.1%**
  * Non-Institutional Agencies: **33.8%**
* **Karnataka Rural:**
  * Institutional Agencies: **67.2%**
  * Non-Institutional Agencies: **32.5%**
  * (Cultivator households in Karnataka: Institutional 68.4%, Non-Institutional 31.3%)
  * (Non-Cultivator households in Karnataka: Institutional 64.3%, Non-Institutional 35.3%)

### 3.3 Interest Rate Distribution of Non-Institutional Debt (Statement 10, Page 97)
In rural Karnataka, outstanding cash debt owed to **Non-Institutional Agencies** carries the following annual interest rate distribution:

| Rate of Interest Bracket | Share of Rural Non-Institutional Debt in Karnataka | Cumulative Share |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nil (Interest-free, relatives/friends)** | 24.4% | 24.4% |
| **< 6% p.a.** | 5.7% | 30.1% |
| **6% - 10% p.a.** | 1.7% | 31.8% |
| **10% - 15% p.a.** | 3.0% | 34.8% |
| **15% - 20% p.a.** | 6.4% | 41.2% |
| **20% - 25% p.a.** | **36.3%** | **77.5%** |
| **25% - 30% p.a.** | 0.8% | 78.3% |
| **30% - 50% p.a.** | **18.9%** | **97.2%** |
| **50% - 100% p.a.** | **2.8%** | **100.0%** |

### Critical Takeaway for Rural Enterprise Advisor:
Over **58.8%** of informal debt in rural Karnataka carries an interest rate between **20% and 100% p.a.** (with 36.3% at 20-25% and 18.9% at 30-50%).
This provides the authoritative, government-certified baseline demonstrating the economic impact of concessional lending schemes (MFS at 6.5%, KCC at 4.0%, PMEGP subsidy) over rural moneylenders.

## 4. Limitations of the Current Document
1. **District-Level Granularity:** The published report (Report 588) disaggregates down to State level and NSS Region level, but does not present standalone tables for individual districts (e.g. Belagavi vs Mysuru).
2. **Enterprise Activity Cross-Tabulation:** The survey divides rural households into "Cultivator" and "Non-Cultivator" (and broad industry in Appendix A), but does not cross-tabulate debt by micro-trade (e.g. Kirana store specifically).
